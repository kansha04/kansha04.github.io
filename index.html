ScriptHelper

A deterministic, hallucination-resistant clinical decision-support assistant delivering verified drug guidance to Zimbabwean pharmacists over Telegram & WhatsApp.

Pharmacists can't quickly get a trustworthy, cited answer about a drug. That means interactions, contraindications, side effects, and dosing, asked at the counter or on the phone. The usual alternatives are general web search, memory, or a general-purpose chatbot. Those are fast, but they can invent or misattribute clinical facts—and in a clinical setting, a confident wrong answer is dangerous.

ScriptHelper, co-engineered with a small team, was built to solve both the trust and delivery problem. Every clinical reply is grounded in verified sources: the FDA’s product labelling, fetched live, and Zimbabwe’s EDLIZ 2020 essential-medicines list. If no source covers the question with sufficient confidence, the assistant declines rather than guesses. Designed strictly as decision-support with full citation traceability, delivered directly over Telegram and WhatsApp.

Built spec-first using GitHub SpecKit and AI assistance—defining strict data contracts and deterministic verification gates upfront before implementing and refining the code under rigorous team review.

The identity seam

Designed the live-read identity layer that decides whether a pharmacist gets a clinical answer. My first design copied KYC results into the licence gate and would have gone stale; analysis showed revocation and expiry had no path through it. I replanned it so the gate reads identity live, and a revoked or expired pharmacist loses access on the next decision.

My story, in my own words.

Where it started

My name is Tinovonga Mushayi. I was born in Zimbabwe in 2008 (currently 18) and moved to South Africa in late 2012. When I was 14, a relative who was a self-taught programmer introduced me to web development, sparking my obsession with tech. I started with HTML and CSS tutorials, building simple static pages that felt like magic at the time.

Leuven

Shortly after, my family relocated to Belgium. I packed my desktop computer into a suitcase, crossed my fingers it would survive the flight, and landed in Leuven, where we live today. Moving to a completely new country was both daunting and electrifying. Learning Dutch and adapting to a new school system took immediate priority, forcing me to put coding on hold for a few months.

The plateau

Determined to go deeper, I worked through courses on Codecademy and freeCodeCamp, picking up JavaScript and Python. But after finishing several tracks, I hit a wall: syntax made sense, but I wasn’t actually learning how software worked. In the summer of 2025, I made a decision to stop grinding tutorial exercises, and start building complex projects that forced me to solve real engineering problems.

Building

I started designing and deploying full projects: a live earthquake tracker powered by the USGS seismic feed (Seismic Core) and a production corporate site for my dad’s consulting firm (AcsysPharma). Seeing how software applied directly to physical and business problems led me to choose Business Supporting Computer Science as my high school track.

This summer (2026)

My biggest step forward: joining a 3-person engineering team to take ScriptHelper from an early prototype into a live clinical pilot. It was my first exposure to production-grade software with clinical stakes. I dove straight in, self-assigned tickets, participated in rigorous code reviews, and learned how real systems are architected under the guidance of seasoned engineers.
